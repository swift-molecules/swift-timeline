# swift-timeline

Use this package when values change over time and you must be able to ask for any instant on its own: an animation rendered frame by frame, out of order or in parallel. Start from `Timeline.Track<Value, Scalar>`, a list of keyframes over a value type you interpolate yourself, and ask it for `value(at:interpolating:)`. Give each keyframe a `Timeline.Easing` for the segment that leaves it: a curve, a cubic Bézier timing curve, or a `Spring` from swift-spring. For a track of `Scalar`s, `value(at:)` mixes straight between keyframes, `Timeline.Track.ramp(at:lasting:easing:)` rises from 0 to 1, `pulses(_:easing:)` gives pulses that die away, and `smooth(at:)` is a smooth curve through the keyframes. Name an easing with `.smooth` or `.power(_:)`, turn any easing around with `reversed` or make it the same from both ends with `symmetric`, or write a timing curve as four numbers with `.bezier(_:_:_:_:)`. Use `Timeline.Track.steps(_:)` for values that jump and hold, `Timeline.Loop` to repeat, and `Timeline.Layer` and `Timeline.Stack` for things that exist over an interval, such as scenes in sequence and an overlay that outlives the cuts between them. Instants are exact `Rational` seconds, so a frame time such as 1001/30000 is not rounded before it is compared. Nothing here keeps state between calls.

## Laws

- A track has at least one keyframe and its keyframes' instants strictly increase. Construction fails with `.empty` or `.order`. `Timeline.Track(constant:at:)` is one keyframe.
- At a keyframe's instant the value is that keyframe's value, exactly, and neither the easing nor the interpolation is called. Before the first keyframe the value is the first value, and from the last keyframe on it is the last value.
- Strictly between two keyframes the value is `interpolate(from, to, progress)`, where the progress is the earlier keyframe's easing applied to the time elapsed since it and the length of the segment, both exact differences of instants approximated once to `Scalar`. The interpolation's typed error is the evaluation's.
- A `Timeline.Easing` is a function from elapsed time and segment duration to progress, in the units the instants are in. `.linear` is elapsed over duration. `.hold` is zero throughout, so the value stays the earlier keyframe's until the next keyframe's instant. `init(fraction:)` makes an easing from a function of elapsed over duration. Progress is passed to the interpolation as it comes, including below zero and above one, so an interpolation must extrapolate if its easings overshoot.
- `Timeline.Easing.bezier(_:)` takes the timing curve as a cubic `Bezier<Point<2, Scalar>>` from (0, 0) to (1, 1) whose two inner control points have x in 0…1, which is what keeps x from turning back. It fails with `.degree` for another number of control points, `.endpoints` for other end points or an inner y that is not finite, and `.monotone` for an inner x outside 0…1. The progress at a fraction is the curve's y where its x equals the fraction, found by bisecting the parameter a fixed number of times with additions and multiplications only, so the same fraction gives the same bits everywhere. At a fraction of zero or less it is 0 and at one or more it is 1. Where the curve's x barely moves, y is only as accurate as that allows.
- `Timeline.Easing.spring(_:velocity:)` is one minus the spring's displacement after the elapsed time, released from one with the given velocity toward the target, in progress per unit of time. It ignores the segment's duration: the spring is not stretched to reach the next keyframe, and if it has not settled by then the value jumps. `Spring.settling(within:from:)` says how long to leave it.
- A track's value at an instant depends on nothing but the track, the instant and the interpolation. Evaluating frames in any order, or one frame alone, gives the same values.
- Keyframes written in source need not be checked by the caller: `Timeline.Track(ordered:)` takes keyframes, or (instant, value) keys with one easing, and traps for none or for instants that do not strictly increase. `steps(_:)` is such keys on `.hold`. The same holds for the other shorthands below: input written in source that breaks their rules traps, while `init(_:)` and `bezier(_:)` throw for input built at run time.
- `Timeline.Easing.bezier(_:_:_:_:)` is `bezier(_:)` of the curve through (0, 0), the two inner points and (1, 1). `.smooth` is 3u² − 2u³ of the fraction u, and `.power(_:)` is u to a whole power of at least one, both by multiplication alone, 0 at a fraction of zero or less and 1 at one or more. An easing's `reversed` is 1 − progress(duration − elapsed, duration), the easing a segment needs to retrace the values of one on the original from its end to its start, and reversing twice gives the original's values up to rounding. Its `symmetric` is progress(2 × elapsed, duration) / 2 over the first half of the duration and 1 − progress(2 × (duration − elapsed), duration) / 2 over the second: one half at the middle.
- For a track whose `Value` is its `Scalar`, `value(at:)` and `value(at:looping:)` interpolate with `from + (to − from) × progress`. `ramp(at:lasting:easing:)` is 0 at the start, rising on the easing to 1 at the start plus the duration, which is positive. `pulses(_:easing:)` is 0 before each pulse, the pulse's peak at its instant, falling on the easing (`.linear` by default) to 0 over the pulse's positive duration, and 0 until the next pulse, which starts after that; no pulses is 0 throughout. The track starts one duration before the first pulse, which is where a loop or `reversed` counts from.
- `smooth(at:)` ignores the easings and passes through each keyframe's value at its instant, exactly. Between two keyframes it is the cubic Hermite curve with the slope at each keyframe that of the line through its two neighbours, and at the first and last keyframes the slope of the line to their one neighbour. Before the first and from the last keyframe on it is the first and last value.
- `map` changes the values and keeps instants and easings. `shifted(by:)` adds an offset to every instant. `stretched(by:)` multiplies every instant's distance from the start by a positive factor and fails with `.factor` otherwise; easings are functions of elapsed time, so a spring easing is not slowed by it.
- `reversed` is the track played backwards over the same span: its value at an instant is the original's value at start + end − instant, for an interpolation that satisfies `interpolate(a, b, p) == interpolate(b, a, 1 − p)`.
- `appending(_:after:)` moves the other track so that it starts a gap after this one ends, and joins the two. The gap must be positive, or it fails with `.order`. Over the gap the value goes from this track's last value to the other's first under the easing of this track's last keyframe; with `.hold` there it is a cut.
- `staggered(count:every:)` is that many copies, the first unchanged and each next one shifted by one more interval.
- A `Timeline.Loop` has a positive period (else `.period`), optionally a count of cycles, and may alternate. `time(at:)` maps a time measured from the loop's start into the first cycle, exactly: the remainder after whole periods, or, when alternating, running back from the period to zero on every second cycle. Zero and negative times are returned as they are. From the end of the last counted cycle on, the time is where that cycle ended: the period, or zero for an alternating loop with an even count. A count beyond `Int.max` is taken as `Int.max`. `value(at:looping:interpolating:)` applies the loop from the track's start.
- A `Timeline.Layer` is alive from the lower bound of its lifetime up to but not at the upper bound. Its `age(at:)` is the instant minus the lower bound, whether or not it is alive then.
- `Timeline.Stack.alive(at:)` gives a `Moment` for each layer alive at the instant, in the order the layers are listed, with its content, age, lifetime and remaining time. `span` runs from the earliest start to the latest end, and is `nil` for no layers.
- `Timeline.Stack.sequence(_:from:overlap:)` gives each part a lifetime of its duration, each starting where the one before ends less the overlap. With no overlap exactly one layer is alive at every instant of the span. With an overlap, two neighbours are both alive for exactly that long before each cut, which is the room a cross-fade needs. It fails with `.overlap` for a negative overlap and with `.duration` for a part that does not last longer than the overlap.
- `Timeline.Stack.stagger(_:duration:from:every:)` gives every content the same duration, each starting one interval after the one before. It fails with `.duration` for a negative duration.
- A layer listed beside a sequence is unaffected by its cuts: its age runs on across them.
