# swift-timeline

Use this package when values change over time and you must be able to ask for any instant on its own: an animation rendered frame by frame, out of order or in parallel. Start from `Timeline.Track<Value, Scalar>`, a list of keyframes over a value type you interpolate yourself, and ask it for `value(at:interpolating:)`. Give each keyframe an `Easing` for the segment that leaves it; the curves are `Easing` from swift-easing, a spring among them. Give a track a `Tail` when its last step has no end, such as a spring that settles toward a value from an instant on: `Timeline.Track(from:at:tail:)` with `.spring(toward:frequency:ratio:)`. `smooth(at:interpolating:)` is a smooth curve through the keyframes of any value you interpolate, a camera pose as well as a number. For a track of `Scalar`s, `value(at:)` and `smooth(at:)` mix straight, `Timeline.Track.ramp(at:lasting:easing:)` rises from 0 to 1, `Timeline.Track.spring(at:frequency:ratio:)` springs from 0 toward 1, and `pulses(_:easing:)` gives pulses that die away. Use `Timeline.Track.steps(_:)` for values that jump and hold, and on a `Timeline.Loop` for a blink. Use `Timeline.instants(_:from:to:weights:)` for events at uneven gaps between two instants, `Timeline.Jitter` for gap weights that are random but the same on every run with one Threefry key, `Timeline.Series` to ask how many of the events have happened by an instant, `Timeline.Loop` to repeat, and `Timeline.Layer` and `Timeline.Stack` for things that exist over an interval, such as scenes in sequence and an overlay that outlives the cuts between them. Instants are exact `Rational` seconds, so a frame time such as 1001/30000 is not rounded before it is compared. Nothing here keeps state between calls.

## Laws

- A track has at least one keyframe and its keyframes' instants strictly increase. Construction fails with `.empty` or `.order`. `Timeline.Track(constant:at:)` is one keyframe.
- At a keyframe's instant the value is that keyframe's value, exactly, and neither the easing nor the interpolation is called. Before the first keyframe the value is the first value, and from the last keyframe on it is the last value.
- Strictly between two keyframes the value is `interpolate(from, to, progress)`, where the progress is the earlier keyframe's easing applied to the time elapsed since it and the length of the segment, both exact differences of instants approximated once to `Scalar`. The interpolation's typed error is the evaluation's.
- A keyframe's curve is an `Easing` from swift-easing, which states its laws; its progress is passed to the interpolation as it comes, so an interpolation must extrapolate if its easings overshoot.
- A track's value at an instant depends on nothing but the track, the instant and the interpolation. Evaluating frames in any order, or one frame alone, gives the same values.
- Keyframes written in source need not be checked by the caller: `Timeline.Track(ordered:)` takes keyframes, or (instant, value) keys with one easing, and traps for none or for instants that do not strictly increase. `steps(_:)` is such keys on `.hold`. The same holds for the other shorthands below: input written in source that breaks their rules traps, while `init(_:)` throws for input built at run time.
- For a track whose `Value` is its `Scalar`, `value(at:)` and `value(at:looping:)` interpolate with `from + (to − from) × progress`. `ramp(at:lasting:easing:)` is 0 at the start, rising on the easing to 1 at the start plus the duration, which is positive. `spring(at:_:velocity:)` and `spring(at:frequency:ratio:velocity:)` are 0 up to their instant and from it on the spring tail toward 1, `Tail.spring(toward:…)`, so they never end and overshoot 1 when the damping ratio is below one. `pulses(_:easing:)` is 0 before each pulse, the pulse's peak at its instant, falling on the easing (`.linear` by default) to 0 over the pulse's positive duration, and 0 until the next pulse, which starts after that; no pulses is 0 throughout. The track starts one duration before the first pulse, which is where a loop or `reversed` counts from.
- A `Tail` is the open last step of a track: a value and a progress that is a function of the time elapsed since the last keyframe alone, in the units the instants are in. Up to and at the last keyframe's instant the tail changes nothing and its progress is not called; after it the value is `interpolate(last value, tail's value, progress(elapsed))`, without end. `Tail.spring(toward:_:velocity:)` is the progress of `Easing.spring(_:velocity:)`, so a damped spring settles on the tail's value and one without damping swings about it for ever; `spring(toward:frequency:ratio:velocity:)` traps where `Spring(frequency:ratio:)` would throw. Without a tail the value holds from the last keyframe on.
- `smooth(at:interpolating:)` ignores the easings and passes through each keyframe's value at its instant, exactly, without calling the interpolation. Between two keyframes it is the cubic Hermite curve with the slope at each keyframe that of the line through its two neighbours, and at the first and last keyframes the slope of the line to their one neighbour. Before the first keyframe it is the first value and from the last on what `value(at:interpolating:)` gives, the tail included. It needs nothing of a value but the interpolation: the cubic is evaluated as a Bézier curve by de Casteljau, at the exact fraction of the segment, and each inner control point is two interpolations of three keyframes' values, one at a progress from one to three halves, so the interpolation must extrapolate. A value that is mixed component by component smooths as its components would alone, exactly. `smooth(at:)` is the same with the straight mix.
- `map` changes the values, the tail's too, and keeps instants, easings and the tail's progress. `shifted(by:)` adds an offset to every instant. `stretched(by:)` multiplies every instant's distance from the start by a positive factor and fails with `.factor` otherwise; easings and tails are functions of elapsed time, so a spring is not slowed by it.
- `reversed` is the track played backwards over the same span: its value at an instant is the original's value at start + end − instant, for an interpolation that satisfies `interpolate(a, b, p) == interpolate(b, a, 1 − p)`. It has no tail, which lies outside the span.
- `appending(_:after:)` moves the other track so that it starts a gap after this one ends, and joins the two. The gap must be positive, or it fails with `.order`. Over the gap the value goes from this track's last value to the other's first under the easing of this track's last keyframe; with `.hold` there it is a cut. This track's tail is cut by what follows and plays no part; the joined track has the other's tail.
- `staggered(count:every:)` is that many copies, the first unchanged and each next one shifted by one more interval. `staggered(count:over:weights:)` shifts the copies by `Timeline.instants(count, from: 0, to: span, weights:)`; with equal weights it is `staggered(count:every:)` at an interval of the span over count − 1.
- `Timeline.instants(_:from:to:weights:)` gives the instants of that many events: the first exactly at the start, the last exactly at the end, and the gap after the k-th event the span times the k-th weight over the sum of the count − 1 weights drawn, in exact `Rational` arithmetic. Only those weights are drawn, so the sequence may be endless; a caller's random numbers in 0..<1, made `Rational`, are one source, a rule for longer gaps another. Instants never go back for an end not before the start, a zero weight makes two events coincide, equal weights give equal gaps, and multiplying every weight by one positive factor changes nothing. One event is at the start and no events gives none. A sequence that runs short, a negative weight, or weights that are all zero trap.
- A `Timeline.Jitter` is weights that stray from one by keyed draws, a sequence without end. Its weight at an index is `1 + spread × (2s − 1024) / 1024`, exactly, where `s` is the top ten bits of the first word of `Threefry.block(key:counter:)` at counter `(index, stream)`: the draw `Threefry.unit(key:stream:index:)` taken down to a multiple of 1/1024 and counted in 1024ths. It lies from `1 − spread` up to, not including, `1 + spread`; the spread is from 0 to 1, else it traps, and the stream is 0 unless given. As a sequence it gives the weights at indices 1, 2, 3 and on, so the gap before event `k`, counting the first as 0, has the weight at `k`. The weights a key gives never change: under key `(0x0123456789ABCDEF, 0xFEDCBA9876543210)` with spread 1/2 the weights at 0 to 4 are 711/1024, 1125/1024, 453/512, 35/32 and 599/512.
- A `Timeline.Series` holds instants that never go back (else `init(ordered:)` traps); `init(_:from:to:weights:)` makes them as `Timeline.instants`. `count(at:)` is how many are at or before an instant, by bisection.
- A blink is `steps([(0, on), (blink, off)])` on a `Timeline.Loop` with a period of two blinks, interpolated by taking the earlier value: on from zero for one blink, off for the next, and on again after each period, exactly; a time before the start is on.
- A `Timeline.Loop` has a positive period (else `.period`), optionally a count of cycles, and may alternate. `time(at:)` maps a time measured from the loop's start into the first cycle, exactly: the remainder after whole periods, or, when alternating, running back from the period to zero on every second cycle. Zero and negative times are returned as they are. From the end of the last counted cycle on, the time is where that cycle ended: the period, or zero for an alternating loop with an even count. A count beyond `Int.max` is taken as `Int.max`. `value(at:looping:interpolating:)` applies the loop from the track's start.
- A `Timeline.Layer` is alive from the lower bound of its lifetime up to but not at the upper bound. Its `age(at:)` is the instant minus the lower bound, whether or not it is alive then.
- `Timeline.Stack.alive(at:)` gives a `Moment` for each layer alive at the instant, in the order the layers are listed, with its content, age, lifetime and remaining time. `span` runs from the earliest start to the latest end, and is `nil` for no layers.
- `Timeline.Stack.sequence(_:from:overlap:)` gives each part a lifetime of its duration, each starting where the one before ends less the overlap. With no overlap exactly one layer is alive at every instant of the span. With an overlap, two neighbours are both alive for exactly that long before each cut, which is the room a cross-fade needs. It fails with `.overlap` for a negative overlap and with `.duration` for a part that does not last longer than the overlap.
- `Timeline.Stack.sequence(_:cuts:)` lays part `i` from `cuts[i]` up to `cuts[i + 1]`, so the parts meet at every cut, as a film laid on its recording is cut where the read says. A part between two equal cuts lives over no time and is never alive. It fails with `.cuts` unless there is one more cut than parts and no cut comes before the one ahead of it. A `Moment`'s `duration` is how long its content lives, as a layer's is.
- `Timeline.Stack.stagger(_:duration:from:every:)` gives every content the same duration, each starting one interval after the one before. It fails with `.duration` for a negative duration.
- A layer listed beside a sequence is unaffected by its cuts: its age runs on across them.
- `snapped(to:)` of a layer or a stack moves each end to the start of the nearest tick of a `Rate` of swift-rate, the later of two as near, which this package re-exports: scenes laid on beats are cut on frames. Two layers that meet still meet, since a shared end snaps to one tick, and a layer shorter than half a tick may come to live over no time.
